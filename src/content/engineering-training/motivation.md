## Motivation

The strongest motivation for my training is progress. There is something almost magical about adding another plate to the bar, moving the pin down on the cable machine, or suddenly being able to do something I could not do before. Getting my first pull-up, my first three dips, and finally holding a good frog stand all gave me an almost indescribable sense of achievement.

<details class="motivation-details">
<summary>Why I keep showing up</summary>

I have never thought of myself as particularly athletic or strong. Growing up, that was always my brother. I only started strength training seriously at 46, and after little more than a year and a half, training has already changed that perception of myself. I have shown myself that I can become stronger and learn difficult physical skills if I work at them consistently. Progress does not require a lifetime of being athletic; it requires showing up, week after week. I can, and I will. That is worth remembering on a cold November morning when staying home seems considerably easier.

Training with other people is another important part of the motivation. Whether I am training with my partner or with Nikolaj, there is both the enjoyment of doing something together and the satisfaction of having someone else notice the progress. Apparently, praise works. Who knew?

Physical changes matter too, even though they are harder to notice from day to day. Progress happens slowly, and I see myself in the mirror every day. But occasionally something makes the accumulated change suddenly visible, such as being able to tighten my belt by another notch. Those moments are enormously satisfying.

Finally, training is an investment in my future health and physical capacity. That is perhaps the least immediate source of motivation because the payoff is decades away, but I know that maintaining strength and muscle as I grow older depends in part on the work I put in now.

Ultimately, I want to be stronger, more capable, and able to keep surprising myself with what my body can learn to do.
