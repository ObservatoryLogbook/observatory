# The Making of a Christmas Calendar

*An Exercise in Love, Logistics and Plausible Deniability*

## 11 October 2026: The Christmas Calendar Problem

The project began on a Sunday morning, at precisely 10:43, with a problem. Christmas was approaching, my partner had already bought me an Advent calendar, and I had absolutely no idea what to give him.

In previous years, I had tried various approaches. One year, I bought him a Reese's Peanut Butter Cups calendar. The following year, I visited Lindt and selected 24 individual chocolates, wrapping and numbering each one myself. I even included two chocolates on the days we would spend together, so we could share them. He loved the idea, particularly the thought that had gone into it.

That gave me an idea. Perhaps the solution wasn't to find a better ready-made calendar, but to make one that was entirely his. Something that reflected his peculiar interests, favourite treats and the things we enjoy doing together.

I started making a list. Protein bars, unusual sweets, Korean skincare, old Danish films, group exercise classes, pumpkin, Christmas decorations and, inevitably, Normal. He loves Normal. I consider entering the shop an act of considerable personal sacrifice.

By lunchtime, we had already worked through four versions of the calendar. There were chocolates and snacks, but also flowers, a massage, an invitation to watch old Danish films and a trip to see the Lucia procession in Copenhagen. I had even found a way to incorporate a Romanian witch.

The calendar was beginning to take shape. What I hadn't anticipated was just how much entertainment the planning itself would provide.

## A Romanian Witch and Other Romantic Inspirations

Some of the ideas were straightforward. Others required a little explanation.

Before we met, my partner had paid a Romanian witch DKK 500 to perform a love spell over FaceTime. He remains convinced that the spell worked, given that we met shortly afterwards and subsequently fell in love. I have never been entirely persuaded by the supernatural explanation, but I cannot deny that the outcome was rather satisfactory.

It seemed only appropriate to acknowledge this remarkable piece of relationship history with a Romanian dinner, prepared by the supposed result of the original spell.

The accompanying card practically wrote itself:

> I 2024 betalte du 500 kr. for at finde kærligheden. Besværgelsen virkede tilsyneladende. Nu får du en rumænsk middag, tilberedt af resultatet.

Other gifts were inspired by smaller observations. He had repeatedly mentioned wanting electric tealights, so those went on the list. He loves the smell of dried oranges at Christmas, which made a little bag of orange slices an obvious choice. And since he enjoys pumpkin almost as much as I enjoy making dreadful jokes, a jar of pumpkin seasoning salt seemed perfect for 15 December, when we were planning to see *Dune 3*.

*The Spice Must Flow!*

I was particularly pleased with that one.

## 7 November: The Shopping Begins

By early November, I had spent considerably more time planning the calendar than actually buying anything. Fortunately, my partner was working on Saturday 7 November, giving me an excellent opportunity to go shopping without having to explain the increasingly strange assortment of products I was looking for.

I came home with eight presents, including a protein bar, Nescafé Double Choc, Simply Dubai Chocolate, ISIS chips, chocolate-covered freeze-dried strawberries, a glass Christmas heart, dried orange slices and the all-important pumpkin seasoning salt.

I also bought wrapping paper, ribbons, gift tags, Christmas cards and miniature baubles to decorate the packages. My previous calendar had involved individually wrapped chocolates, but this was becoming a considerably more elaborate production.

The experience vouchers needed special attention. I wanted them to sound like something I would actually say, rather than generic gift certificates.

For our visit to Normal, I wrote:

> Kærlighed kræver ofre. Store ofre.
>
> Jeg inviterer dig på en tur i Normal. Ja. Frivilligt.
>
> Du får 50 kr. til lige præcis det, du har lyst til, og jeg lover at følge med rundt uden at brokke mig. Ret meget.
>
> Hvis det ikke er ægte kærlighed, ved jeg snart ikke, hvad der er. ❤️

The massage voucher promised a highly dedicated, if somewhat unqualified, personal masseur. And the invitation to watch an old Danish film included a solemn promise not to suggest *Star Trek* instead.

By the end of the weekend, the first presents were wrapped and numbered. The project had finally become something physical, rather than a collection of increasingly elaborate ideas.

There were still a few gaps in the calendar, though, and the final present remained a mystery.

## The Great Whimsical Negotiations

Finding a suitable present for Christmas Eve proved surprisingly difficult. I initially considered buying something for our home, but this required navigating a fundamental difference in our approaches to interior design.

I like Japandi. Natural materials, restrained colours and relatively uncluttered surfaces.

My partner likes *whimsical*.

For some time, I struggled to understand exactly what that meant. We had bought some glittery Christmas stars, and apparently the darker ones were whimsical while the lighter ones were not. An elaborate, colourful forest in a *Hunger Games* film was also whimsical. I was beginning to suspect that the term covered a rather broad range of aesthetic phenomena.

Then he bought a starlight projector and discovered that his favourite setting filled our living-room ceiling with moving red and blue light. He was delighted. I felt as though our apartment had been temporarily converted into a galactic nightclub.

I nevertheless attempted to find a compromise. A small mushroom-shaped lamp seemed promising: a little magical, but still sufficiently restrained to coexist with my preferred interior design. After some research, I found a Jotex FUNGHI lamp, just 15 cm tall, for DKK 199.

For a while, it looked like the perfect solution.

Then came the parrots.

My partner sent me pictures of a green vase covered in colourful parrots. It cost DKK 1,299, and his accompanying message was:

> *gisp* den er perfekt

My response was somewhat less enthusiastic.

**GYSELIG!!!**

Friends suggested that it could stand in a quiet, discreet corner. I thought the basement would be ideal.

The exchange was tremendously useful, however, because it finally demonstrated just how different our interpretations of whimsical could be. I began to suspect that the carefully restrained mushroom lamp was more my idea of a compromise than his idea of a wonderful present.

So the lamp was abandoned.

And Christmas Eve was once again without a gift.

## 21 November: Two Secrets and a Scheduling Problem

By 21 November, several of the experience vouchers had been written and wrapped, including two that I had been planning since 11 October.

The first was an invitation to attend a SATS group exercise class together.

That very day, my partner went to a group class and absolutely loved it. Afterwards, he told me how much fun it would be if we tried one together. I responded as casually as I could:

*"Det ser vi lige til..."*

What he didn't know was that the invitation was already waiting for him in a numbered Christmas package.

The second secret involved the Lucia procession in Nyhavn on 13 December. I had planned this outing weeks earlier and wanted to make sure he wouldn't accept an evening shift that day.

Fortunately, we had already agreed to visit some of Copenhagen's pastry shops over the winter. During the summer, I had accompanied him on various ice cream outings, despite not being particularly enthusiastic about ice cream myself. Now we were going to explore something more to my taste.

So I suggested reserving 13 December for a pastry shop visit.

There was, of course, no pastry shop visit planned for that day.

A couple of hours later, he sent me a very apologetic message. He had accepted an extra shift on 13 December, from 8 am until 2 pm, and felt terrible about interfering with our plans.

I was delighted.

The shift would finish with plenty of time to spare before the Lucia procession, and the surprise remained completely intact. Naturally, I couldn't tell him that, so I graciously accepted his apology.

Sometimes being a considerate boyfriend requires a surprising amount of deception.

The actual invitation was already written:

> Lys i mørket ❤️
>
> Jeg inviterer dig på en lille juleudflugt til Nyhavn, hvor vi skal se Luciaoptoget på vandet.
>
> Jeg sørger for noget varmt at drikke. Du sørger for at holde mig varm.
>
> Glædelig 13. december, skat ❤️

I was rather pleased with the arrangements. Both surprises had been planned long before he independently suggested doing something very similar, and I had somehow managed not to reveal either of them.

## The Great Tank Top Expedition

The last unresolved problem was the Christmas Eve present.

After abandoning the mushroom lamp, I decided to look for something more practical. My partner wears tank tops almost every day, often underneath his colourful shirts, and I happen to think he looks particularly good in them. A nice tank top seemed like an excellent idea.

Unfortunately, finding the right one turned out to be considerably more complicated than expected.

He prefers a slightly loose fit, often with narrow shoulder straps and a ribbed texture. We considered various colours, including dark green, grey and brown. I even examined three of his existing tank tops to establish the correct size, only to discover that they came from different brands and were labelled L, XL and possibly M.

Armed with this remarkably inconclusive information, I went to Field's.

I visited Zara, H&M, New Yorker, Kaufman, Tøjeksperten, Magasin and Bilka.

Seven shops.

Not one suitable tank top.

Apparently, Danish retailers consider November an inappropriate time to sell men's sleeveless clothing. This is unfortunate when the intended recipient wears it all year round.

Eventually, I admitted defeat and turned to online shopping. There I found a three-pack from Pier One in dark grey, dark blue and dark green. Since he already owned a tank top from the same brand, it seemed like a sensible choice.

I ordered size L.

The total was DKK 173, including delivery.

After extensive discussions about mushrooms, parrots, garment construction and the seasonal limitations of Danish retail, the final Christmas present had been found.

Three tank tops.

I was absurdly pleased.

> Jeg ville egentlig have pakket min bagdel ind til dig, men det viste sig at være lidt upraktisk.

It may not be the most conventional Christmas sentiment, but I suspect he will understand.

## A Note for You

If you're reading this, you've presumably opened all 24 presents and discovered that your Christmas calendar was the result of a rather extensive planning exercise.

It began on 11 October, when I realised that I wanted to make something more personal than another ready-made calendar. I wanted to give you little things I knew you would enjoy, but also invitations to spend time together and a few reminders of the things that make our relationship ours.

Some of the presents were inspired by conversations we had months earlier. Others came from things you mentioned in passing, without knowing that I was quietly taking notes. And on at least two occasions, you suggested doing something together when I had already written and wrapped an invitation for exactly that.

Keeping those secrets was not always easy.

Along the way, I learned considerably more about your interpretation of whimsical, discovered that seven shops can collectively fail to sell a perfectly ordinary item of clothing, and found a surprisingly useful Christmas present in the story of a Romanian witch.

I also discovered that making a calendar like this is rather more work than simply buying one.

But I enjoyed it. Every bit of it.

Because the point was never to give you 24 expensive or impressive presents. It was to give you 24 little reminders that I notice the things you like, that I remember our conversations, and that I love spending time with you.

I hope the calendar made you smile.

And I hope reading about its creation makes you smile again.

I love you.
