## Method

### Preparation

1. Finely slice the pointed cabbage.
2. Place the cabbage in a large bowl with a little salt and lemon juice. Massage briefly to soften it slightly.
3. Dice the cucumber, halve the cherry tomatoes and chop the red peppers.
4. Finely slice the red onion and chop the parsley.
5. Combine the prepared vegetables in a large serving bowl.
6. Drain and rinse the chickpeas, then pat them dry.
7. Cut the halloumi into slices approximately 1 cm thick.

### Skyr dressing

1. Combine the skyr, lemon juice, garlic and olive oil.
2. Season with salt and black pepper.
3. Add a little water if necessary to achieve a pourable consistency.
4. Refrigerate until serving.

### Cooking

1. Heat a little olive oil in a large frying pan.
2. Add the chickpeas, smoked paprika, cumin, salt and pepper.
3. Fry for approximately 6–8 minutes, stirring regularly, until heated through and lightly browned.
4. Remove the chickpeas from the pan.
5. Heat the pan again and fry the halloumi slices until golden on both sides.
6. Add the chickpeas and halloumi to the prepared salad.
7. Scatter the parsley over the top and serve with the skyr dressing.

## Notes

- The cabbage can be prepared ahead of time. Briefly massaging it with salt and lemon helps soften its texture.
- The vegetables and dressing can also be prepared in advance.
- Halloumi is best served immediately after frying.
- If the salad is being prepared for a later meal, keep the halloumi separate and fry it shortly before serving.
- The chickpeas can be reheated separately if necessary.
- The dressing should be stored in the refrigerator until needed.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- The salad was successful, with a fresh flavour and good overall balance.
- The combination of chickpeas, halloumi, cabbage and skyr dressing worked well.
- Halloumi fried several hours before serving became rubbery and less pleasant to eat.
- The dish is therefore less suitable for preparing completely in advance.

### Future improvements

- Fry the halloumi immediately before serving rather than preparing it several hours ahead.
- For meals that must be fully prepared in advance, test a different protein component that retains a softer texture after storage.
