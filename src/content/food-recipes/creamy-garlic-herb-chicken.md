## Method

### Preparation

1. Cut the chicken into large, evenly sized bite-sized pieces.
2. Season lightly with salt and black pepper.
3. Finely chop the onions and garlic.
4. Peel the carrots and cut into thin half-moons.
5. Remove the outer leaves and core of the pointed cabbage, then shred finely.
6. Chop the parsley and set aside.

### Browning the chicken

1. Heat the olive oil in a very large frying pan or heavy-bottomed pot over medium-high heat.
2. Brown the chicken in batches to avoid overcrowding.
3. Cook for approximately 2–3 minutes per side, until lightly golden.
4. The chicken does not need to be fully cooked at this stage.
5. Transfer to a plate and set aside.

### Cooking the vegetables

1. Reduce the heat to medium and melt the butter in the same pan.
2. Add the onions and carrots.
3. Cook for approximately 6–8 minutes, stirring occasionally.
4. Add the garlic and cook for another minute.
5. Add the shredded pointed cabbage in batches, allowing each batch to soften slightly before adding more.
6. Cook for approximately 5–8 minutes, until the cabbage has reduced considerably in volume but still retains some texture.

### Making the sauce

1. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir thoroughly.
2. Cook for approximately 1 minute.
3. Gradually pour in the stock, stirring continuously to prevent lumps.
4. Add the cooking cream, thyme and Dijon mustard, if using.
5. Bring to a gentle simmer.
6. Simmer for approximately 5 minutes, stirring occasionally.

### Finishing the dish

1. Return the browned chicken and any accumulated juices to the pan.
2. Stir gently to coat the chicken in the sauce.
3. Simmer for approximately 8–12 minutes, or until the chicken is fully cooked and reaches 74°C internally.
4. Add the frozen peas and cook for another 3–5 minutes, until heated through.
5. Stir in the chopped parsley.
6. Add lemon juice gradually, tasting after each addition.
7. Adjust the seasoning with salt and black pepper.
8. If the sauce is too thick, add a little stock or water. If too thin, simmer uncovered briefly.

### Serving

Serve hot, either on its own or with rice or boiled potatoes.

## Notes

- The dish is intended to be vegetable-rich, with pointed cabbage providing much of the volume.
- Browning the chicken separately develops flavour without overcooking the vegetables.
- Add the cabbage in batches if the pot is not large enough to accommodate it all at once.
- The sauce should lightly coat the chicken and vegetables rather than resemble a thin soup.
- Dijon mustard is optional and should provide background savouriness rather than a pronounced mustard flavour.
- Lemon juice should be used sparingly to balance the creaminess without making the dish taste strongly of lemon.
- The peas should be added towards the end to preserve their colour and texture.
- The dish can be refrigerated and reheated gently. Add a little water or stock if the sauce thickens during storage.
- Avoid boiling vigorously when reheating, as this may toughen the chicken.

### Future improvements

- Evaluate whether the balance between chicken and vegetables is satisfactory.
- Assess whether 300 ml cooking cream and 400 ml stock produce the desired sauce consistency.
- Determine whether the sauce benefits from the optional Dijon mustard and lemon juice.
- Evaluate whether the cabbage retains sufficient texture after simmering.
- Assess whether the dish needs additional garlic or fresh herbs.
- Consider replacing some of the cooking cream with blended white beans in a future version to increase fibre and create a naturally creamy sauce.
