## Method

### Preparation

1. Drain and rinse the white beans and chickpeas.
2. Set aside approximately 300 g of the white beans for blending later.
3. Finely chop the onions and garlic.
4. Dice the red peppers and courgettes into bite-sized pieces.
5. Wash and prepare the spinach or kale.
6. Chop the parsley and set aside for serving.

### Building the flavour base

1. Heat the olive oil in a very large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat.
2. Add the chopped onions and cook for approximately 6–8 minutes, until softened and beginning to turn golden.
3. Add the diced red peppers and cook for another 5 minutes.
4. Add the courgettes and continue cooking for 4–5 minutes.
5. Stir in the garlic and cook for approximately 1 minute.
6. Add the tomato purée, sweet paprika, oregano, cumin and chilli flakes.
7. Cook for another 1–2 minutes, stirring continuously, until the tomato purée darkens slightly and the spices become fragrant.

### Simmering the stew

1. Add the chopped tomatoes and 400 ml vegetable stock.
2. Stir thoroughly, scraping any browned bits from the bottom of the pot.
3. Add the remaining white beans and all the chickpeas.
4. Bring to a gentle simmer.
5. Cook uncovered for approximately 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally.
6. Add additional stock if the stew becomes too thick.

### Creating a creamy texture

1. Transfer the reserved 300 g white beans to a blender.
2. Add approximately 150–200 ml of the hot tomato sauce from the pot.
3. Blend until completely smooth, adding a little more liquid if necessary.
4. Return the blended mixture to the stew.
5. Stir thoroughly and simmer gently for another 5 minutes.

### Adding the greens

1. Add the spinach or kale.
2. If using spinach, cook for approximately 3–5 minutes until wilted.
3. If using kale, simmer for approximately 8–10 minutes until tender.
4. Season with salt and black pepper.
5. Add lemon juice gradually, tasting as you go.

### Lemon and garlic skyr cream

1. Combine the skyr, finely grated garlic and lemon juice in a small bowl.
2. Season lightly with salt.
3. Add a little cold water until the mixture has a soft, spoonable consistency.
4. Refrigerate until serving.

### Serving

1. Spoon the hot stew into deep bowls.
2. Crumble the feta over the top.
3. Add a generous spoonful of lemon and garlic skyr cream.
4. Finish with chopped fresh parsley.
5. Serve immediately.

## Notes

- The recipe is intended to produce a substantial, thick stew rather than a thin soup.
- Blending some of the white beans creates a naturally creamy sauce without dairy or coconut milk.
- The feta and skyr cream are added at serving to provide contrast with the warm tomato and bean mixture.
- Add the lemon juice gradually. The tomatoes already contribute acidity.
- Use sweet paprika rather than smoked paprika.
- For a deeper flavour, allow the tomato purée to cook briefly before adding the liquid.
- The stew can be prepared in advance and reheated gently. Add a little water or stock if it thickens during storage.
- Keep the feta and skyr cream separate when storing leftovers.

### Future improvements

- Evaluate whether the balance between white beans and chickpeas is satisfactory.
- Assess whether blending 300 g of white beans produces the desired creamy texture.
- Determine whether the tomato sauce needs additional sweetness or acidity.
- Evaluate the seasoning, particularly the balance of paprika, oregano, cumin and chilli.
- Consider adding a little extra hot paprika for greater depth without making the dish particularly spicy.
- Assess whether the feta and skyr cream provide sufficient contrast to the stew.
