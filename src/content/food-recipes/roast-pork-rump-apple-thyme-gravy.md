## Method

### Preparation

1. Remove the pork from its packaging and pat dry with kitchen paper.
2. Season generously with salt, black pepper and thyme.
3. Wash the potatoes and cut into evenly sized wedges.
4. Preheat the oven to 200°C fan.
5. Prepare the onion and apple for the gravy.

### Roasting the pork

1. Heat the cooking oil in a large frying pan.
2. Brown the pork on all sides over medium-high heat.
3. Transfer to an ovenproof dish or roasting tray.
4. Insert a meat thermometer into the thickest part.
5. Roast until the centre reaches approximately 65°C, following any specific cooking instructions supplied with the joint.
6. Remove from the oven, cover loosely and allow to rest for 10–15 minutes.
7. Reserve the roasting juices for the gravy.
8. Slice the pork thinly across the grain immediately before serving.

### Roasted potatoes

1. Toss the potato wedges with olive oil, thyme, salt and black pepper.
2. Spread over one or two baking trays without overcrowding.
3. Roast at 200°C fan for approximately 35–45 minutes, turning halfway through.
4. Continue until the potatoes are tender inside and golden with crisp edges.

### Apple and thyme gravy

1. Melt the butter in a saucepan over medium heat.
2. Add the finely chopped onion and cook until softened.
3. Add the grated apple and cook for another 2–3 minutes.
4. Pour in the stock and add the apple cider vinegar and thyme.
5. Simmer gently for approximately 10–15 minutes.
6. Add the reserved roasting juices from the pork.
7. Blend the sauce until smooth.
8. Stir in the cooking cream if using.
9. Simmer briefly to adjust the consistency.
10. Season with salt and black pepper. Add a little more vinegar if the sauce needs acidity.

### Vegetables and serving

1. Steam the green beans until tender but still slightly firm, or lightly sauté the pointed cabbage.
2. Slice the rested pork.
3. Serve with the roasted potatoes, vegetables and apple and thyme gravy.

## Notes

- Pork rump is leaner than pork neck and should not be cooked using the same long, slow method as pulled pork.
- Use a meat thermometer rather than relying exclusively on cooking time.
- The suggested internal temperature is approximately 65°C. Resting may increase the temperature slightly.
- The gravy is intended to be savoury with a gentle apple sweetness and noticeable thyme, rather than a strongly sweet apple sauce.
- Add the pork roasting juices before the final seasoning, as they may contain salt.
- The optional cooking cream should round out the sauce without making it resemble a heavy cream casserole.
- The pork and potatoes may require different oven timings. Start the potatoes early or finish them while the pork rests.
