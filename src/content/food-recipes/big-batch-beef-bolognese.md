## Method

### Preparation

1. Chop the mushrooms and onions.
2. Finely dice or grate the carrots.
3. Finely chop the garlic.
4. Measure out the remaining ingredients.

### Cooking

1. Heat a little oil in a large, heavy-based saucepan.
2. Brown the minced beef, working in batches if necessary.
3. Add the onions, carrots and mushrooms. Cook until the vegetables have softened and the mushrooms have released some of their moisture.
4. Add the garlic and tomato purée. Stir and cook briefly.
5. Add the chopped tomatoes and 200 ml water or stock.
6. Season with oregano, thyme, balsamic vinegar, salt and black pepper.
7. Bring to a gentle simmer.
8. Simmer for at least 45 minutes, stirring occasionally.
9. Taste and adjust the seasoning. Add a little extra liquid if the sauce becomes too thick.

### Serving with pasta

1. Cook 300 g protein pasta according to the packet instructions.
2. Serve with approximately half of the prepared Bolognese.
3. Finish with grated Parmesan.

Reserve the remaining sauce for another meal.

## Notes

- Designed as a large batch that can provide the basis for two different dinners.
- The original plan was to serve half with protein pasta and reserve the remainder.
- The sauce was subsequently used successfully as the filling for [Mexican Loaded Potatoes](/engineering/food/mexican-loaded-potatoes/).
- For the loaded potatoes, approximately 1.5–1.6 kg prepared sauce was combined with 500 g drained black beans.
- The quantities of oregano, thyme and balsamic vinegar were not recorded precisely.
- Cool any reserved sauce promptly. Refrigerate for short-term use or freeze for longer storage.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- The sauce worked successfully as a versatile base for more than one meal.
- Approximately 1.5–1.6 kg prepared sauce provided a suitable filling for Mexican Loaded Potatoes when combined with black beans.
- The combination of beef, tomatoes and vegetables was suitable for batch cooking.
