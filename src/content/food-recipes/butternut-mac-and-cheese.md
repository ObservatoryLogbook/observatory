## Method

### Preparation

1. Preheat the oven to 200°C fan.
2. Peel and deseed the butternut squash, then cut into chunks.
3. Peel and chop the carrots.
4. Peel and roughly chop the onions.
5. Prepare the garlic cloves.
6. Clean and slice the mushrooms.
7. Grate the Cheddar and Parmesan, keeping the quantities for the sauce and topping separate.

### Cooking

1. Steam the butternut squash, carrots and onions for approximately 20–25 minutes, until completely tender.
2. Meanwhile, fry the mushrooms in a little oil until they have released their moisture and begin to brown.
3. Cook the protein pasta in salted water for approximately 2 minutes less than the packet instructions. Drain.
4. Transfer the steamed vegetables to a blender.
5. Add the cottage cheese, skimmed milk, garlic, cornflour, Dijon mustard, paprika, lemon juice, salt and black pepper.
6. Blend until the sauce is completely smooth.
7. Transfer the sauce to a large saucepan and warm gently, stirring regularly.
8. Add 100 g Cheddar and 50 g Parmesan. Stir until the cheese has melted and the sauce is smooth.
9. Taste and adjust the seasoning.
10. Combine the pasta, cheese sauce, fried mushrooms and frozen peas in a large ovenproof dish. Mix thoroughly.
11. Sprinkle the remaining 150 g Cheddar and 25 g Parmesan evenly over the surface.
12. Bake for approximately 20–25 minutes, until the topping is golden and the filling is piping hot.

## Notes

- The recipe uses 1.2 kg prepared butternut squash, excluding peel and seeds.
- The vegetables are steamed before blending to create a smooth sauce.
- Cottage cheese and skimmed milk provide the base of the sauce.
- The cheese is divided between the sauce and topping: 150 g total in the sauce and 175 g on top.
- Slightly undercooking the pasta prevents it becoming excessively soft during baking.
- The original recipe produces a large quantity of food.
- The exact quantities of lemon juice and paprika were not recorded.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- The finished dish had a good, creamy consistency.
- The flavour was too sweet, with butternut squash dominating the cheese.
- The sauce needed more salt, acidity and savoury depth.
- Approximately 1 kg remained after dinner and tasted surprisingly good cold the following day.

### Future improvements

- Reduce the quantity of butternut squash and carrots to make the sauce less sweet.
- Replace some of the squash with cauliflower to retain volume and creaminess.
- Increase the acidity and adjust the salt more assertively.
- Test a small quantity of Worcestershire sauce or light miso to increase savoury depth. Check that the Worcestershire sauce is vegetarian if required.
- Do not simply increase the cheese quantity: the main issue is the balance between sweetness, acidity and savoury flavour.
- Keep the original recipe unchanged until a revised version has been tested.
