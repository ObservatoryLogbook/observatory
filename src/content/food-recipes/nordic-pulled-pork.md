## Method

### Preparation

1. Cut the onions into large wedges and dice the apples.
2. Prepare the garlic.
3. Place the onions, apples and garlic in the bottom of the slow cooker.
4. Combine the apple juice, stock, apple cider vinegar, tomato purée, thyme and sweet paprika.
5. Season the pork with salt and black pepper.
6. Place the pork on top of the onions and apples.
7. Pour the braising liquid around the meat.

### Slow cooking

1. Cover the slow cooker and cook on LOW for approximately 8–10 hours.
2. Continue cooking until the pork is sufficiently tender to pull apart easily with two forks. Actual cooking time depends on the slow cooker and the thickness of the meat.
3. Remove the pork from the slow cooker.
4. Shred the meat using two forks.
5. If desired, blend some of the cooked onions, apples and cooking liquid to create a thicker sauce.
6. Taste the sauce and adjust with salt or apple cider vinegar if necessary.
7. Return the shredded pork to the sauce, adding enough liquid to keep the meat moist without making it excessively wet.

### Serving

Serve with:

- [Apple-Braised Red Cabbage](./apple-braised-red-cabbage.md)
- [Skyr Buns](./skyr-buns.md)

The pork can also be served in ordinary soft bread rolls.

### Reserving meat for another meal

1. Before serving, reserve approximately 650–750 g of cooked pulled pork.
2. Include sufficient cooking liquid to keep the reserved meat moist.
3. Cool the reserved portion promptly.
4. Refrigerate for short-term use or freeze for later use.

The reserved pork is intended for a separate pulled pork pie with a potato and cauliflower mash topping.

## Notes

- Pork neck fillet contains sufficient fat and connective tissue to become tender during slow cooking.
- The apple and onion mixture provides both flavour and a base for the sauce.
- The recipe deliberately avoids a sticky American-style barbecue sauce.
- Sweet paprika was used instead of smoked paprika in the first preparation.
- Retain the cooking liquid, particularly when reserving pork for another meal.
- The precise cooking time and final sauce consistency should be documented after the first preparation.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- The first preparation used 2 tsp sweet paprika instead of the originally proposed smoked paprika.
- The final flavour, texture and sauce consistency have not yet been evaluated.

### Future improvements

- Assess the balance between apple sweetness, acidity and thyme after the first tasting.
- Adjust the sauce consistency if necessary.
- Record the actual slow-cooking time and final cooked meat yield.
