## Method

### Preparing the vegetables

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C fan.
2. Grease a large, deep ovenproof dish, approximately 30 × 40 cm.
3. Peel the potatoes and cut into cubes approximately 1.5–2 cm across.
4. Boil the potatoes in lightly salted water for 8–10 minutes, until almost tender but still holding their shape.
5. Drain thoroughly and allow the potatoes to steam dry.
6. Heat the olive oil in a large frying pan.
7. Add the chopped onions and cook over medium heat for approximately 5 minutes.
8. Add the sliced mushrooms and increase the heat slightly.
9. Fry for 8–10 minutes, until most of the released moisture has evaporated and the mushrooms begin to brown.
10. Add the spinach and cook until wilted and any excess moisture has evaporated.
11. If using frozen spinach, thaw it completely and squeeze out as much liquid as possible before adding it to the pan.
12. Remove the vegetables from the heat.

### Preparing the egg mixture

1. Blend the cottage cheese until smooth.
2. Crack the eggs into a very large bowl and whisk until combined.
3. Add the blended cottage cheese, salt, black pepper and oregano, if using.
4. Whisk until the mixture is reasonably uniform.
5. Crumble the feta into the mixture and stir gently.

### Assembling the frittata

1. Distribute the cooked potatoes evenly across the prepared baking dish.
2. Spread the mushroom, onion and spinach mixture over the potatoes.
3. Pour the egg mixture evenly over the vegetables.
4. Gently move the ingredients around with a spoon or spatula to ensure that the egg mixture reaches the bottom of the dish.
5. Make sure the vegetables are distributed evenly and that no large pockets of dry ingredients remain.

### Baking

1. Place the dish on the middle shelf of the oven.
2. Bake at 180°C fan for approximately 35–50 minutes.
3. Check the centre after 35 minutes. The frittata should be set rather than liquid in the middle.
4. If the surface browns too quickly, cover loosely with aluminium foil.
5. Continue baking until the centre reaches approximately 74°C.
6. Remove from the oven and allow to rest for 10 minutes before cutting into portions.

### Serving

Serve warm, cut into generous squares.

The frittata can be served on its own or accompanied by a simple green salad or a spoonful of skyr dressing.

## Notes

- This is a substantial, meal-sized frittata rather than a small breakfast dish.
- The recipe is designed for a large ovenproof dish. A smaller or deeper dish will require a longer baking time.
- Thoroughly draining the potatoes and removing moisture from the mushrooms and spinach is essential.
- Blending the cottage cheese produces a more uniform egg mixture without noticeable curds.
- Feta contributes considerable salt, so adjust the seasoning accordingly.
- The frittata should be cooked through but not baked longer than necessary, as overcooking may make the eggs rubbery.
- The vegetables can be prepared in advance and refrigerated separately.
- If preparing ahead, cool the cooked vegetables promptly and refrigerate until needed.
- Refrigerate leftover frittata promptly and reheat until piping hot.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- First tested on 16 November 2026.
- The actual preparation used 1,800 g potatoes, 450 g cottage cheese and 750 g spinach.
- The planned 250 g feta was omitted.
- The remaining ingredients followed the original recipe.
- The frittata was considered slightly dry by one taster, while the other particularly enjoyed the texture.
- The recipe produced a very substantial quantity, with enough leftovers to replace the following day's planned dinner.

### Future improvements

- Consider reducing the potatoes to 1,200–1,500 g to improve the balance between the egg mixture and the vegetables.
- Test whether a shorter baking time produces a softer texture.
- Consider retaining the increased cottage cheese quantity for additional moisture.
- Evaluate whether adding feta improves the flavour and perceived richness.
- Record the actual baking time in a future test.
