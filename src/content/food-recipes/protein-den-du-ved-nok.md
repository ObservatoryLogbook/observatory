## Method

### Preparation

1. Preheat the oven to 175°C conventional heat.
2. Line a 20 × 20 cm baking tin with baking parchment.
3. Measure out the cake ingredients.
4. Prepare 100 ml strong coffee for the topping and set aside.

### Cooking

1. Combine the skyr, eggs, skimmed milk, Sukrin Gold and liquid sweetener in a mixing bowl.
2. Mix until reasonably smooth.
3. Add the flour, whey protein powder, cocoa powder, baking powder and salt.
4. Stir until the dry ingredients are incorporated and the batter is uniform. Avoid unnecessary prolonged mixing.
5. Transfer the batter to the prepared tin and spread evenly.
6. Bake for approximately 20–24 minutes, until the cake is set. Avoid overbaking, as the protein-rich batter can become dry.
7. Remove from the oven and allow to cool slightly while preparing the topping.

### Chocolate and coconut topping

1. Melt the butter gently in a saucepan.
2. Add the strong coffee, cocoa powder, Sukrin Gold and a pinch of salt.
3. Stir until the mixture is smooth and well combined.
4. Add the liquid sweetener and stir thoroughly.
5. Fold in the desiccated coconut until evenly coated.
6. Spread the topping across the cake, making sure it reaches the edges.
7. Allow the topping to settle before slicing.

## Notes

- Use a 20 × 20 cm tin to preserve the tested cake thickness.
- The cake contains 250 g skyr and 50 g whey protein powder, giving it a denser texture than a conventional sponge cake.
- The tested version uses 20 ml Salling liquid sweetener in total: 10 ml in the cake and 10 ml in the topping.
- The sweetness depends on the specific liquid sweetener. Other products may have different concentrations and should not be substituted millilitre for millilitre without adjustment.
- The topping quantities are deliberately generous and should cover the entire cake.
- The topping is an important part of the finished cake, not merely a decorative layer.

## Testing and development

### Observations

- Version 1.0 had a good, deep chocolate flavour, but the topping quantity was insufficient and the sweetness was too low for the preferred result.
- The texture was denser and less airy than a conventional chocolate sponge, but remained acceptable.
- Version 2.0 doubled the topping and increased the sweetness.
- Version 2.0, with 10 ml liquid sweetener in both the cake and topping, was a major success.
- The richer topping and increased sweetness resolved the main criticisms of the first version.

### Future improvements

- No changes are currently required to the successful version 2.0.
- Any future attempt to produce a lighter sponge should be tested separately, without replacing the established recipe.
