## Method

### Preparation

1. Cut the beef into large, evenly sized cubes, approximately 4-5 cm.
2. Pat the beef thoroughly dry with kitchen paper and season with salt and black pepper.
3. Roughly chop the onions and finely chop the garlic.
4. Peel the carrots and cut into thick slices, approximately 1-1.5 cm.
5. Clean the mushrooms and cut into halves or quarters, depending on their size.

### Browning the beef

1. Heat the olive oil in a large, heavy-bottomed casserole over medium-high heat.
2. Brown the beef in several batches, leaving sufficient space between the pieces.
3. Allow the beef to develop a deep brown crust before turning.
4. Transfer each batch to a plate and set aside.
5. Retain the browned residue at the bottom of the casserole, as it will contribute flavour to the sauce.

### Building the sauce

1. Reduce the heat to medium.
2. Add the chopped onions to the casserole and cook for approximately 5-7 minutes, stirring occasionally.
3. Add the garlic and tomato purée.
4. Cook for another 1-2 minutes, stirring continuously.
5. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir thoroughly.
6. Gradually pour in the red wine, scraping the bottom of the casserole to release the browned residue.
7. Simmer the wine for approximately 5 minutes to reduce slightly.
8. Add the beef stock, dried thyme, bay leaves and Worcestershire sauce.
9. Return the browned beef and any accumulated juices to the casserole.
10. Stir gently and bring to a very gentle simmer.

### Slow braising

1. Cover the casserole with a lid.
2. Reduce the heat to low and simmer gently for approximately 2½-3½ hours.
3. Check occasionally to ensure that the liquid is barely simmering rather than boiling vigorously.
4. Stir gently from time to time and add a little water or stock if the sauce reduces excessively.
5. After approximately 2 hours, begin checking the beef for tenderness.
6. When the beef is approaching tenderness, add the carrots and continue cooking for approximately 40-45 minutes.

### Preparing the mushrooms

1. While the beef is braising, melt the butter in a large frying pan over medium-high heat.
2. Add the mushrooms in batches if necessary.
3. Fry until their released moisture has evaporated and they develop a golden-brown surface.
4. Season lightly with salt and black pepper.
5. Set aside until the final stage of cooking.

### Finishing the stew

1. Add the browned mushrooms approximately 20 minutes before the stew is ready.
2. Continue simmering gently until the beef is very tender and the carrots are cooked.
3. Remove the bay leaves.
4. Taste and adjust the seasoning with salt and black pepper.
5. If the sauce is too thin, remove the lid and simmer gently for another 10-15 minutes.
6. If the sauce is too concentrated, add a little water or beef stock.
7. Allow the stew to rest for approximately 10 minutes before serving.

### Serving

Serve hot with mashed potatoes, boiled potatoes or crusty bread.

The stew can also be served on its own, as it contains a substantial quantity of beef and vegetables.

## Notes

- Use beef suitable for slow braising, such as chuck or blade. Lean, quick-cooking cuts are not suitable substitutes.
- Cut the beef into large pieces to help retain moisture during the long cooking time.
- Browning the beef thoroughly is important for developing a deep, savoury flavour.
- Avoid overcrowding the casserole when browning the meat.
- The stew should simmer very gently. Vigorous boiling may cause the beef to become dry and the sauce to reduce too quickly.
- Cooking time depends on the cut and size of the beef. Tenderness is more important than following an exact time.
- Add the carrots towards the end of braising to preserve their shape and texture.
- Frying the mushrooms separately helps develop their flavour and prevents them from becoming watery.
- The red wine contributes acidity and depth. A dry, medium-bodied wine is preferable to a sweet wine.
- Worcestershire sauce adds savoury complexity without dominating the flavour.
- The stew can be prepared in advance and refrigerated for up to 3 days. Cool promptly and refrigerate within 2 hours.
- Reheat thoroughly and gently, adding a little stock or water if necessary.
- The stew is suitable for freezing.

### Future improvements

- Evaluate whether 400 ml red wine provides the desired balance of acidity and richness.
- Assess whether 700 ml beef stock produces the correct sauce consistency after braising.
- Determine whether the sauce benefits from additional reduction at the end of cooking.
- Evaluate whether the carrots retain sufficient texture when added during the final 40-45 minutes.
- Assess whether separately browned mushrooms provide sufficient depth of flavour.
- Consider adding a small amount of balsamic vinegar if the finished sauce needs additional acidity.
- Consider increasing the mushroom quantity in a future version if a higher vegetable-to-meat ratio is desired.
