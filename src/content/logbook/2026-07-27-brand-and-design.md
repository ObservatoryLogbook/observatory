date: 2026-07-27

Today was all about defining the Observatory's brand and visual identity. The implications are surprisingly wide.

The front page, the actual Observatory, received a complete design overhaul. These changes are part of making it future-proof, allowing me to gradually introduce dashboards, projects, and other parts of the Observatory over time. This is my landing page. From here, I navigate the entire Observatory, including the Logbook, which is now an integrated part of it rather than the website itself.

In order to do this, it was important to establish some fundamental design principles. I wanted something minimalistic with a distinct Japandi feel. Everything on the page serves a purpose, even elements that exist primarily to support the overall aesthetic, such as the Lucide icons. The colour palette and typography were also chosen to create a calm atmosphere. This should be a place where I genuinely want to spend time, even when life gets busy or complicated.

The project is rapidly maturing and turning into something that feels at least semi-professional. I am making design decisions that I have never had to make before, which is both exciting and challenging for a former astrophysicist who is used to thinking in terms of right and wrong. Now it is more a question of how right—or how wrong—something feels. It challenges me in a very good way, and I definitely want to continue that.

The Observatory is no longer simply a website. It is becoming the place from which I organise, explore, and revisit my own observations.
