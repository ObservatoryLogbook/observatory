The day began with a consultation about a tiny broken blood vessel on my face and somehow very quickly expanded into suggestions that I might also want to do something about my eyelids, the bags under my eyes and the small line between my eyebrows.

Apparently I had accidentally booked a consultation about being old and tired.

I declined the opportunity to have my entire face improved and went for brunch at The Union Kitchen instead. That was considerably nicer, although I managed to forget to mention my honey allergy and subsequently discovered raw honey in the granola. Not my finest piece of planning.

The afternoon was mostly spent in Observatory, where Cardiovascular went from an empty page to something surprisingly close to a first complete version. Blood pressure now has weekly observations derived from the three individual measurements, a 3-week average, measurement ranges, clinical reference ranges, a 365-day history, hover interaction and 12-week trends. Along the way, more of the generic time-series machinery escaped from Body Composition into a shared module, which feels like exactly the kind of architecture that should emerge from actual use rather than anticipation.

And then there was everything happening outside Observatory.

A message earlier in the day had revealed that a rather large decision had apparently been understood rather differently by two people. That produced several hours of a slightly unpleasant feeling in my stomach while I waited for the evening and tried, with some success, to distract myself by building blood-pressure graphs.

Fortunately, the evening brought conversation rather than catastrophe. What had felt alarmingly consequential turned out to be a misunderstanding.

Some days have a theme. This one mostly had range.
