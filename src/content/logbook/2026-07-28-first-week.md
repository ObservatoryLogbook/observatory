---
title: First week of observations
date: 2026-07-28
---

It has now been about a week since I started building Observatory, and it feels like a good moment to pause and reflect.

The original idea was simple: I wanted an online place to call home. Somewhere to collect my daily observations, my larger projects, and eventually more advanced data visualisations and other tools that I build for myself.

I have never been particularly fond of social media. Setting up an Instagram, Bluesky, or X profile would certainly have been the easiest solution, but none of them would ever have felt like home. They are excellent platforms for publishing content, but they are ultimately someone else’s place. I also explored more traditional website solutions such as WordPress templates, but they felt equally impersonal.

That is how this project began.

My background is in astrophysics, so computers, scripting, and programming have never been intimidating. Quite the opposite: they are things I genuinely enjoy. Once I discovered Astro, I decided it was worth learning, even if it meant starting from almost nothing in the world of modern web development. The last time I developed a website from scratch was more than twenty years ago, using basic html. 

What began as a technical project quickly became a design project as well. The difficult questions were no longer about Astro, but about how I wanted to interact with my own work. How should observations be organised? How should data be presented? Most importantly, what should this place feel like so that I would genuinely want to return to it again and again? Unlike astrophysics, there is rarely a single correct answer. Much of the process became one of judgement, experimentation, and iteration.

One week later, I am genuinely proud of what has emerged. The Logbook is now fully functional and has begun to feel like the backbone of Observatory. There are still improvements to make under the hood (the RSS feed is not quite where I want it to be), but it is remarkable how quickly the project has evolved from a loose collection of ideas into something that feels coherent. More importantly, it has already started to feel like home.

I will take a few days away from developing Observatory itself, but I will continue recording observations and ideas as they come to me. When I return, the next step will probably be either building the first dashboards or creating dedicated homes for some of my larger projects. I am not entirely sure which direction Observatory will grow first, and I rather like that.

Development may pause for a few days, but observations rarely do.
