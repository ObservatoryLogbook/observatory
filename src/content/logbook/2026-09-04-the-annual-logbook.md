title: The annual logbook
date: 2026-09-04
---

The annual Fanø trip started in 1999 — or so we believe. It is an annual discussion, and by now one of those rituals that belong to the trip. We do know that at one point we decided that the annual trip started in 1999. Which is now 27 years ago. We had a small break of a couple of years, so we’re almost sure this year’s edition is the 24th.

The reason we know we decided the trip started in 1999? We’ve kept a journal ever since. The early years just had a few keywords describing the trip and the participants, but now it is an elaborate document with pictures describing the trip, the weather, which games we played, what we had to eat and drink, and any notes for next year’s trip. It’s our own annual logbook.

And so, who are the participants? We are four guys who met in high school, became friends, and the rest is history. We all see the trip as an annual short break where we leave our everyday lives behind and are essentially back in high school with our little friend group. We’ve seen each other go through highs and lows, and I don’t think anything can surprise any of us anymore when it comes to each other. After all, even though we all hate to admit it, we’re approaching 50. And we’ve reached the age our parents were when we started going to Fanø.

This morning started in Copenhagen. Although we’re always eager to keep a lot of traditions, we’re also happy to try new things. This time we tried buying our dinner in Copenhagen — a full-sized octopus — instead of close to the island. And we didn’t buy any fish where we usually do; instead, we bought it all on the island.

After driving for nearly five hours, we finally arrived, and the trip always starts with a nice lunch with herring (smoked and pickled), warm-smoked halibut, and fish balls. Accompanied by lots of snaps and beer.

Then we sat and talked, went for a walk — the weather was terrible, so we almost didn’t make it out of the house — and played some cards while enjoying a few gin and tonics before cooking the octopus for dinner. After a late dinner, we played some more cards and talked, had hot dogs as a late-night snack, and were in bed by 1:30am. Not too bad!

A few traditional things, a few minor new things, and the guys are just like themselves. Which is the most important thing!
