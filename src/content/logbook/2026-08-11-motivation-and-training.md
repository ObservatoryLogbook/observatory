title: Motivation and training
date: 2026-08-11
---

What motivates me to train four times a week?

That was the key question of the day. I want to keep the answer somewhere central on the Training Engineering page, so that even on a cold November morning, I have something I can quickly look at that will get me out the door.

Training has become a significant part of my life. I actually enjoy going to the gym, and I particularly enjoy seeing the progress I make. And of course I track that progress: the weight I lift, the number of reps I can do, and the goals and milestones I set along the way. I enjoy pushing my body, and I am constantly amazed by what it can learn to do.

It wasn't always like this. I only started exercising regularly at around 35, about 13 years ago. At the time, I lived in Cambridge, MA, and I would see runners on the streets everywhere. Eventually, I decided that I wanted to try it myself. I downloaded a Couch-to-5K app and followed it religiously for the nine weeks it took to reach 5K. And I made it. I ran 5K for the first time in my life. It may not sound like much, but for me it was a huge achievement.

Since then, I have completed numerous 5K races and several 10K races, including the Royal Run, where I ran my fastest 10K two years ago in just under 50 minutes. I have even completed a half marathon. Needless to say, I enjoy running.

But running is not enough. A year and a half ago, at the ripe old age of 46, I decided to focus more seriously on strength training. I had done some Pilates and yoga, but I wanted something more. So I set myself a goal: I wanted to do a pull-up.

I quickly realised that the gym is a maze, and I had no clue where to start. ChatGPT could design a training programme for me without any problems, but I had no idea whether I was actually doing the exercises correctly. Instead, I decided to get help from a personal trainer. It is certainly the luxury option, but it is also remarkably effective. I still work with him today.

I achieved my pull-up goal in just over six months. Since then, I have achieved my goal of doing unassisted dips. I am now on my way to deadlifting 150 kg and bench pressing 100 kg, and by the end of the year my goal is to do a handstand. Being able to push my body like this is an amazing feeling. I get a little stronger, I can push myself a little further, and I keep setting new strength goals because I keep achieving the ones I have already set.

The main reason I am able to achieve these goals is self-discipline, which leads to consistency. I aim to be in the gym four times a week, and at least twice when life gets in the way. This matters particularly when I am mentally tired from work and motivation is low. I train in the morning before work, when I am strongest and most focused.

But I also realise that it doesn't take much to fall out of the habit. If that happens, my greatest fear is that I won't start again. Losing something that has become such an important part of my life would be disastrous.

So I keep coming back to my motto:

*I can, and I will. Just keep showing up.*
