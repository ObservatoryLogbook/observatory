---
title: Force Majeure
date: 2026-10-10
---

Almost two years in the making.

For nearly two years, we have been developing, refining and preparing an entirely new evaluation process. Much of it had to be invented from scratch. There were plans, revisions, decisions, uncertainties, and a seemingly endless number of details that needed to come together.

And this week, finally, it happened.

Five days of intense meetings, scientific discussions, dinners and very long working hours. Monday and Tuesday both ended with me getting home around 22:30, and Thursday and Friday followed much the same pattern. Wednesday was supposedly the quieter day, although it mostly disappeared into catching up on everything else.

Not everything went according to plan. Of course it didn’t. This was the first time we had done any of this, and some things inevitably had to be figured out on the fly. But the process worked. The meetings worked. The discussions worked.

**We actually did it.**

And not merely in the sense that everything was completed.

The scientific discussions were extraordinary. I learned an enormous amount, not least from listening to researchers engage seriously with work outside their own immediate areas of expertise. Seeing those conversations unfold, and knowing how much thought had gone into creating the conditions for them, was immensely satisfying.

The feedback was equally rewarding. We received so much praise from the participants, and it meant a great deal to me. After almost two years of building something without knowing exactly how it would work in practice, having experienced researchers tell us that it worked well was profoundly reassuring.

Then there were the evenings.

Wonderful food, excellent company, and long conversations with thoroughly lovable nerds. After days of formal discussions, the dinners offered something different: the pleasure of talking science with people who genuinely love it.

Friday was particularly special. The physicists were there.

My people.

Somewhere in the middle of the week, a colleague and I also had an excellent meeting with external advisers about another major responsibility that lies ahead. A task that had felt rather undefined suddenly became much more manageable. It was reassuring to discover that we could approach it systematically, with the right expertise and preparation.

But that was only a small part of the week.

The real story was the culmination of those two years.

By Friday evening, I was running on very little sleep. Four consecutive nights with barely six hours each, long days, and almost no time for anything beyond work. Observatory had been completely neglected. Even writing a short observation felt impossible.

And yet I wouldn’t have wanted to miss any of it.

Now it is Saturday. The meetings are finished, the work has been delivered, and the exhaustion has finally arrived in full. My head feels extraordinarily heavy, my patience is practically nonexistent, and I have already decided that sleeping may be the most important activity of the weekend.

Next week should be quieter. Perhaps I will even go home early a few days.

But beneath the exhaustion is something much more important.

Relief. Satisfaction. Pride.

We built something from the ground up. We saw it come to life. And it worked.

**We fucking did it.**
