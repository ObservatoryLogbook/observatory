The consolidation phase continues.

After spending much of Monday concluding that Observatory had finally reached the point where I could spend a little less time building the telescope and a little more time looking through it, I seem to have taken the idea rather seriously. Yesterday I added 14 recipes and 13 books. This morning I finished adding my recipes to Food. Tonight it was finally time for Science.

I returned to Apartment Interior, which has technically existed as a Science project for a while, but until now its project page has been almost embarrassingly empty. That was particularly silly because the actual project is anything but empty. I have been thinking about, buying and arranging furniture, lighting, colours, materials and objects since moving into the apartment.

What started as an attempt to create something resembling a perfect Japandi catalogue interior has also changed considerably along the way. When I moved in, my things rather inconveniently moved in with me. And many of those things matter.

There are LEGO flowers from people I love, NASA posters, a glass sculpture from my former postdoc supervisor, Japanese grumpy toads, my partner's small wooden seal from his late mother and various other objects that would probably fail a particularly strict Japandi inspection. Removing them would make the apartment more stylistically consistent, but considerably less ours.

That realization has clarified the project surprisingly well. I still want the calm that originally attracted me to Japandi: oak and other natural materials, muted colours, relatively few objects, visual order and enough empty space to let the apartment breathe. But the aesthetic is increasingly becoming a framework rather than a rulebook. The calm surroundings should give the meaningful, nerdy and occasionally strange things room to exist, rather than exclude them.

The apartment itself has also become part of the design brief. The large west-facing windows, the changing light and the uninterrupted view across the park and golf course are still among my favourite things about living here. Even after two years, I am drawn towards the view when I enter the living room. I would generally rather watch the sunset than the television, so there is little point in designing the room as though the furniture were its most important feature.

The project page now reflects much more of this thinking. It has a material and colour direction, a growing set of design principles and a small visual design notebook for decisions currently under consideration. The first of those is, naturally, the dining table: restrained oak and linoleum versus a considerably more expressive solid-oak table with knots and imperfections. There are opinions in the household.

Adding the design notebook also resulted in a couple of small improvements to the project-page styling. Images now behave like reference images rather than enormous catalogue photographs, and tables have been given a quieter Observatory treatment. I ended up putting the individual reference sections inside expandable details, allowing the material to be there without letting it take over the entire page.

What I particularly like about tonight's work is that almost none of it required new architecture. Apartment Interior already had somewhere to live. Markdown could already contain everything I wanted to write, and even the visual notebook only required a few small CSS adjustments once real content exposed what was missing.

That is exactly how I hoped this next phase of Observatory might work: use the system, fill it with the things it was built to contain, and let actual use tell me what needs to be developed next.

The telescope works. There is quite a lot to look at.
