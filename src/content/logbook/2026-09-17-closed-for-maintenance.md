I really like training with my new squat shoes. I started using them a week ago, and I feel like I get deeper into my squats, my lower back is less exposed, and my strength is slowly returning. Finally! My patience has been tried and tested over the past two and a half months since that fateful July 1st, when I first developed lower-back pain. It feels good to be back.

However, the training session didn't go quite as planned. The beginning was completely off, in fact. I upgraded my Apple devices to iOS 27 yesterday, with near-fatal consequences.

I have a shortcut on my phone that starts Strength Training on my Apple Watch and opens everything I need for the session. It takes an Apple Note from my training programme template, renames it with today's date and copies it into my training log folder. From there, I can enter weights, reps and RPEs as I go. When I'm done, I have another shortcut that closes everything down. It also takes the latest training log, copies it back into the template folder and renames it appropriately so it can be reused the following week.

Brilliant!

Except that after yesterday's update, the shortcut could no longer open Apple Fitness. So this morning I had to start everything manually. Something is clearly closed for maintenance in Cupertino.

Work was an experience in and of itself, as usual, with an extraordinarily sweet conclusion. As mentioned, we turn 150 next week, which will be celebrated with cake. Today we got to sample it.

It is *Det Gyldne Tårn* from the venerable Copenhagen patisserie La Glace, and it was already one of my favourites. I may accidentally have had two pieces.

Still, there is nothing quite like concluding the day with a bit of Observatory.

Tonight we expanded Sick Bay with an entirely new section for circumferences. Until now, the dashboard has focused on body composition from my daily KaradaScan measurements. But once a week, usually on Sunday morning, I also measure my shoulders, chest, waist, hips, upper arm and thigh.

We decided not to show all of those measurements individually. My arm and thigh circumferences barely change and would make for spectacularly boring plots. Instead, Sick Bay now focuses on two quantities: waist circumference and a derived metric we call Form index, defined as `(shoulders + chest) / (waist + hips)`.

Both are now included in Current status, complete with twelve-week trends. Below that is a new two-panel history plot covering the past year. Raw weekly measurements are shown as faint points, while a centered three-measurement average gives the eye something calmer to follow. The line breaks if there are more than fourteen days between measurements, so we do not pretend to have continuity where there is none.

And, because yesterday's body-composition hover worked so well, the circumference plot now has the same interaction. Moving across the chart gives a shared vertical hairline and shows the nearest actual measurement, with the raw waist and Form index values from that date.

One thing became increasingly obvious while building all of this: Sick Bay contains almost no explanatory text. I actually like that. It feels like an instrument panel rather than a report. So instead of interrupting the graphs with explanations, we added a small expandable Methodology section at the very bottom. It documents the instruments I use, how often I measure things, how the derived metrics are calculated, and how trends and smoothing are handled.

Closed by default, naturally.
