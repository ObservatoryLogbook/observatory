---
title: Unforgettable days
date: 2026-09-25
---

Some days are just meant to be unforgettable. The day I left home to spend a year in Japan as an exchange student at the age of 18. The day I got my Master’s degree. The day I got my Ph.D. The first date with my partner. These days are like lighthouses throughout life.

Not surprisingly, today was such a day.

My workplace turned 150. The celebrations had been more than three years in the making. They began with a formal ceremony in a local park in Copenhagen, where we laid a wreath in honour of the discoverer of electromagnetism, H. C. Ørsted. He was an inspiration to our founder, and it is fair to say that, without H. C. Ørsted and his public lectures, our organisation would not be here today.

The celebrations continued with a gigantic layer cake made by Copenhagen’s oldest surviving pâtisserie, La Glace. It was fantastic! I may accidentally have had three pieces… but one of them was after lunch.

For the evening, the highlight of the celebrations, we took a bus to Frederiksborg Castle in North Zealand. From the moment we arrived, there was a very special atmosphere. The men wore tuxedos and the women wore long gowns. Everyone had dressed for the occasion. The castle has a bell tower, and the bells were playing tunes specially chosen to celebrate the day. Upon entering, we were immediately treated to a special anniversary beer brewed specifically for this evening. We had about an hour to mingle, with violinists walking among the guests, delicious canapés, and so on. Incredibly festive.

The dinner itself took place in the Great Hall of the castle, and it was truly spectacular. We started with a men’s choir singing songs from 150 years ago in celebration of science and beer. Then came the unveiling of his first official portrait as king. I was seated almost directly opposite him, which felt very special. There were speeches, a short film celebrating interdisciplinary research centres, more musical entertainment, and of course absolutely delicious food, accompanied by beers brewed specifically to match the dishes. The company was excellent, the atmosphere truly festive. The evening ended back in the hall where we had started, but now with all the beer you could drink and an assortment of petits fours.

At the end of the day, I was full. Full of impressions, of high spirits, of celebration, of feeling special, of feeling lucky to experience such an event. And I was tired. I’d had another bad night’s sleep the night before, but that couldn’t take away from how special this whole thing felt. Thank you!
