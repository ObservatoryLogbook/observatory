---
title: Finally getting something done
date: 2026-09-11
---

I slept for almost nine hours last night, but somehow managed not to get a particularly good night's sleep. After yesterday's stomach problems, I was awake on and off from around 3am until my alarm went off at 5:20.

So, no training this morning.

Instead, I went to work, where I somehow managed to spend almost the entire day talking to people. From 9:45 until 15:30, I was in meetings or on the phone more or less continuously.

On a Friday!

That should probably be illegal.

The strange thing about days like this is that I actually get a lot done. Decisions are made, problems are solved, people are aligned, and things move forward. But because I haven't *made* anything, it doesn't really feel like I have accomplished anything. Apparently, six hours of productive conversation do not satisfy whatever primitive part of my brain keeps track of whether I have done a day's work.

At least the evening was very different.

I finally had a proper evening at home with my partner. After his evening shifts earlier this week and my spectacularly early bedtime yesterday, we hadn't seen much of each other. We spent part of the evening fantasising about where to go for our winter holiday in February. Southeast Asia is currently rather high on the list.

And then I finally got something done!

Over the past weeks, I have slowly been working my way through the handwritten measurements from my KaradaScan. Tonight, I finished the job. Every measurement since I got the scale on July 6 last year — weight, body fat percentage and muscle percentage — has now been digitised.

No interpolation. Missing days remain missing. Just the original observations, finally turned into structured data.

And that means something rather exciting: tomorrow, we can start putting real data into Supabase.

For the past couple of weeks, Sick Bay has contained nothing but fictional Captain Picard body measurements while I built and tested the infrastructure around them. Authentication works. Permissions work. Reading and writing work. The test data can appear and disappear exactly when they should.

Now the foundations are ready.

Tomorrow, Sick Bay gets its first real patient.

Now *that* feels like getting something done.
