When we set out to make Observatory, one of the initial goals was to produce nice-looking dashboards, where I could track my training progress. I had already experimented with something like that in Apple Numbers, which worked fine in the sense that I could track progress. However, visually speaking it was lacking. 

I am sure that I could spend days and days wrangling with Numbers to make it all look just right, but I didn't want to do that. And as the idea of Observatory started crystallizing, it became clear that dashboards would become an integral part of Observatory. 

In the beginning the idea was to have Dashboards be their own thing in Observatory, but after a week or two, it became clear that this approach did not feel natural. It did not make sense to have a page about Training, and then have to go somewhere else to see the corresponding Dashboard - the two belonged together. 

Today, everything finally started coming together in a near-complete form. Over the past eight days, I have noted down my goals, the current focus, my motivation for training, the current context of my training, and then two days ago, we made the status cards. Today it was time to make the plots showing and highlighting the evolution, and why it is so important to just keep showing up. 

The Performance Charts, as we dubbed them, show the change in estimated 1 Rep Max over time for each of the three main exercises: squat, bench press, and deadlift. Furthermore, the trend over the last 56 days (8 weeks) is shown in each chart. 

For me it was important that the charts tell a story, so I don't have to update long figure captions. They now do exactly that with the little trend lines and quantitative results. Furthermore, it is easy to spot gaps in my training history, which are most often is due to injury, training-related or otherwise. 

One thing I noticed about myself: as Observatory is becoming more and more streamlined and professional-looking, in my own humble opinion, I find that I need to restrain myself from throwing more colors into it, more emojis, perhaps even more gifs, Geocities style. That is not Observatory, but I am beginning to miss at least a dash of muted color here and there. 

That is for the future, though.
